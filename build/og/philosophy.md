# The Clearing

A visual philosophy for things that are small, patient and hungry.

The Clearing believes the most persuasive picture is a picture of absence. Not the thing, but the space a thing has made: a field of growth with one calm hollow in it, and at the centre of the hollow, the creature responsible. Meaning lives in the gradient between thick and thin, between crowded and cleared. Nothing is labelled because the field itself is the evidence. The eye reads the story the way it reads footprints in snow.

Space is organised on a strict halftone grid, every mark the same distance from its neighbours, so that the only thing allowed to vary is size. Growth swells the marks until they almost touch; consumption shrinks them back to specks. The grid is never broken and never decorated. Its discipline is what makes the irregular living shape inside it legible, the way graph paper makes a hand-drawn curve look deliberate. Every mark must sit exactly on its lattice point; the work should feel meticulously plotted, the product of painstaking, patient placement.

Colour is a single ecosystem with four registers: the near-black of deep earth, the warm brown of turned soil, the olive of young moss, and one acid chartreuse reserved for the thickest growth. A fifth, a soft flesh pink, appears exactly once, as a living accent. The palette never borrows from outside itself. Light is not depicted; it is implied by where the chartreuse gathers.

Scale works in two voices. One is monumental and rounded, set large and warm, friendly enough to be spoken aloud. The other is a whisper, small and tracked wide, a cataloguer's annotation in the corner. Between them sits a generous margin of quiet. Type is never a paragraph; it is a gesture placed with master-level restraint, aligned to the same unseen grid as the marks.

Composition favours asymmetry with a firm horizon. The ground is a continuous scalloped line, the one curved edge in an otherwise lattice world, and the creature rests on it rather than floating. Weight sits low and to one side; the eye enters at the words, travels across the thinning field and lands on the clearing. The whole should look as if it took countless hours: every edge crisp, every spacing considered, nothing crowding the frame, the calm confidence of deep expertise.
